How routing works
Every claim needing manual intervention carries a diagnosis code, a dollar value, a provider type and a plan. The platform reads those, matches them to the certification each processor actually holds, ranks what is left by days remaining on the statutory clock, and pushes each processor a personal worklist through distribution services. A processor opens their list and everything on it is theirs to work. Nothing arrives that they are not allowed to touch.
What changed
Why the BI platform did this
The vendor demonstrated their own application for this. Everything it needed already lived in the warehouse behind the claims reporting: the open queue, the codes, the dollar values, the plan rules and the processor certifications. Distribution services were built to push a report to the right person on a schedule, and a worklist is a report with a name on it. Using them to assign work is not what the tool was designed for, but it is squarely what the tool is good at, and it shipped in days rather than in a procurement cycle.
Outcome

Sample data shaped to a real operation. The statutory clock is 30 days from receipt, after which the plan pays a penalty · a mis-routed open is a processor opening a claim they are not certified to work and putting it back · queues are certifications, not teams: a processor can hold more than one · time to close is receipt to final determination.
